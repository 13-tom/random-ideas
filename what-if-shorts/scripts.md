# "WHAT IF...?" — 3D Animation Shorts Scripts

Format: vertical 9:16, 30–45 seconds each.
Structure of every short: **Hook (0–3s) → Disaster (3–15s) → Science / What really happens (15–35s) → Twist + Survival tip (35–45s) → Loop line.**

Tip: keep the main character the same in every video (e.g. a simple 3D guy named "Bob" with a big head) so viewers recognise your channel.

---

## 1. What If Your Lift's Cable Snaps?

| Time | Visual (3D animation) | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob in a lift, 20th floor. A loud *SNAP*. Lights flicker. Freeze frame on his face. | "What if your lift cable snaps… at the 20th floor?" |
| 3–8s | X-ray view: the cable whips upward, lift starts dropping. Floor numbers fly past: 19… 18… 17… | "Most people think you'd fall straight to the ground." |
| 8–18s | Camera zooms to the side rails. Metal brake clamps shoot out and grip the rails, sparks fly. Lift stops at floor 14. | "But every lift has a speed sensor called a governor. If the lift falls too fast, it fires safety brakes that clamp onto the rails — within a few metres." |
| 18–26s | Cutaway: lift actually has 4–8 cables, Bob counts them on fingers. | "And a lift doesn't hang on one cable. It has several — each one strong enough to hold the full lift alone." |
| 26–36s | Bob tries to jump right before impact (slow-mo, comic). Red ❌ on screen. | "And no, jumping at the last second won't save you. You'd still be falling at the same speed as the lift." |
| 36–45s | Bob calm, pressing the alarm button, sitting on the floor. | "If you're ever stuck: stay calm, press the alarm, and never try to climb out. The lift is the safest place to be." |
| End | Text: "Would you still take the stairs?" | "Comment: lift or stairs?" |

**SFX:** cable snap, metal screech, sparks, heartbeat during the fall.

## 2. What If Your Car Brakes Fail at 100 km/h?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob driving down a mountain road. Presses brake — pedal sinks to the floor. | "What if you press the brake… and nothing happens?" |
| 3–8s | Speedometer climbing: 80… 90… 100. Sharp turn ahead. | "Your car is speeding downhill. You have about 10 seconds." |
| 8–14s | Step 1 graphic. Bob pumps the brake pedal fast. | "Step one: pump the brake pedal hard and fast. It can build back some pressure." |
| 14–20s | Close-up of gear stick, shifting down 5→4→3. Engine RPM rises. | "Step two: shift to a lower gear. The engine itself starts slowing the car." |
| 20–27s | Bob slowly pulls the handbrake — not yanking it. | "Step three: pull the handbrake slowly. Yank it, and your car can spin out." |
| 27–34s | Red ❌ over Bob turning the key off. Steering wheel locks. | "And never switch off the engine — your steering can lock and power steering goes away." |
| 34–42s | Car rubs gently against a sand bank / road barrier and stops. Dust cloud. | "Still too fast? Scrape along a barrier or steer into an uphill or soft ground. Turn on hazard lights and honk." |
| End | Bob breathing heavy, giving thumbs up. Text: "Save this. It could save your life." | "Share this with someone who drives." |

## 3. What If a Plane Door Opens Mid-Flight?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Plane at 35,000 ft. A passenger grabs the emergency door handle. | "What if someone opens the plane door mid-flight?" |
| 3–10s | Bob pulls with all his strength. Door doesn't move. Pressure arrows push from inside onto the door. | "Good news: they can't. At cruising height, air pressure pushes the door shut with the force of several tonnes." |
| 10–18s | Door shape explained: plug-shaped door, bigger than the frame, like a cork. | "Most plane doors are shaped like a plug — bigger than the hole — so the inside pressure seals it tighter." |
| 18–30s | "WHAT IF it did break?" — window blows out, papers fly, oxygen masks drop. | "If a hole does appear, air rushes out, it gets freezing cold, and oxygen masks drop. You have only seconds of useful consciousness." |
| 30–40s | Bob puts mask on himself first, then helps a kid. | "That's why they say: put your own mask on first." |
| End | Text: "Seat belt ON at all times." | "This is why you keep your seat belt on." |

## 4. What If You Fall Into a Lake With Your Car?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Car skids off a bridge and splashes into water. | "What if your car falls into water? You have about 60 seconds." |
| 3–10s | Water rising inside. Bob tries to open the door — won't move. | "Don't try the door. Water pressure outside makes it almost impossible to open." |
| 10–20s | Big text: S – W – C – O. Seatbelt off, Window, Children, Out. | "Remember S-W-C-O: Seatbelt off. Window open or broken. Children first. Out!" |
| 20–30s | Bob smashes side window at the corner with a headrest metal pole / window breaker tool. | "Electric windows work for a short time — open them immediately. If not, hit the corner of the side window, not the middle." |
| 30–40s | Bob swims up, kid on his back. | "Don't wait for the car to fill up. Get out while it's still floating." |
| End | Text: "Keep a window breaker in your car." | "Tag someone who needs to see this." |

## 5. What If You Stop Sleeping?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob staring at a phone, clock spinning, "Day 1". | "What if you stopped sleeping completely?" |
| 3–10s | Day 1: dark circles, yawning, eats junk food. | "After 24 hours: your brain works like you're slightly drunk." |
| 10–18s | Day 2: Bob forgets his own phone in his hand. | "48 hours: your brain starts taking 'micro-sleeps' — seconds of blackouts without you noticing." |
| 18–28s | Day 3: walls melting, shadow figures. | "72 hours: hallucinations. You may see things that aren't there." |
| 28–38s | Brain cartoon with trash piling up. | "Your brain cleans its waste while you sleep. No sleep — no cleaning." |
| End | Bob finally falls asleep on the keyboard. Text: "Go to sleep." | "So… why are you still scrolling? Go sleep." |

## 6. What If You Get Struck by Lightning?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob in an open field, holding an umbrella. Hair stands up. | "If your hair suddenly stands up in a storm… you have seconds." |
| 3–12s | Lightning strike in slow-mo, 30,000°C label. | "A lightning bolt is about 5 times hotter than the surface of the sun." |
| 12–22s | X-ray: current flows over skin, burn pattern (fern shape) appears. | "Most people actually survive — the current often flows over your skin and can leave fern-shaped marks." |
| 22–32s | Myth ❌: hiding under a tree. Tree gets hit, Bob flies. | "Never hide under a tall tree. Get inside a building or a car with a metal roof." |
| 32–40s | Bob runs into a car, lightning hits car, he's safe. | "A car's metal body sends the current around you into the ground." |
| End | Text: "When thunder roars, go indoors." | "Follow for more 'What If' videos." |

## 7. What If You Fall Off an Escalator / Your Clothes Get Stuck?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob's shoelace gets pulled into the escalator gap. | "What if your shoelace gets stuck in an escalator?" |
| 3–12s | Close-up of the comb plate at the top, lace getting pulled. | "Escalator steps disappear into a comb at the top. Loose laces, sarees and slippers can get caught." |
| 12–22s | Zoom to a red emergency stop button at the bottom/top. | "Every escalator has a red emergency stop button — at the top and the bottom. Most people never notice it." |
| 22–32s | Someone presses it, escalator stops. | "Shout for someone to press it — or press it yourself if you see it happen to someone else." |
| 32–40s | Tips montage: stand in the middle, hold the handrail, tie laces. | "Stand in the middle, away from the edges, and hold the handrail." |
| End | Text: "Did you know about this button?" | "Comment if you've ever seen this button." |

## 8. What If You Swallow a Chewing Gum?

| Time | Visual | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Bob accidentally swallows gum. Mom shouts "It'll stay in your stomach for 7 years!" | "Does swallowed gum really stay in your stomach for 7 years?" |

| End | Text: "Myth BUSTED." | "Which myth should I bust next?" |

## Production checklist (for each short)

1. **Hook in first 2 seconds** — show the disaster immediately, not after an intro.
2. **Big on-screen text** for each step (many people watch without sound).
3. **Slow-motion + freeze frame** at the scariest moment.
4. **X-ray / cutaway views** to explain the science — this is what makes 3D shorts look premium.
5. **Same character** in every short for channel identity.
6. **End with a question** to boost comments, and make the last frame loop into the first.
7. **Disclaimer in caption:** "For awareness only. In a real emergency, call your local emergency number (India: 112)."

**Tools:** Blender (free) for 3D, Mixamo for character animation, CapCut for text/captions/sound effects, ElevenLabs or your own voice for VO.
